Optimal capacity allocation and constraints

The optimizer minimizes average local travel over spatial assignments. If qᵢ is the local share assigned to clinic i and fᵢ is its flexible arrival rate, it requires λL qᵢ + λF θᵢ + fᵢ ≤ μᵢ, q₁ + q₂ = 1, f₁ + f₂ = λC, and nonnegative shares and flows. The entered rates are used directly.

ClinicLocal rateFixed rateFlexible rateTotal assigned rateService capacity

Flexible patients have zero travel cost in this model. Their split above is one feasible optimal allocation; other splits can have the same minimum travel. Extra flexible demand can leave local travel unchanged while it fits within the remaining capacity.

This is a long-run capacity-feasible travel bound. Finite simulation estimates can fall below it through sampling or backlog accumulation.

Optimization, interpretation, and critical-load comparison

At the entered rates, the optimizer minimizes local travel subject to λL qᵢ + λF θᵢ + fᵢ ≤ μᵢ, q₁ + q₂ = 1, f₁ + f₂ = λC, and nonnegative shares and flows. It supplies clinic weights δᵢ(λ), normalized to have minimum zero, so that local patients minimize dᵢ + δᵢ(λ). These are the same entered-rate weights used by the travel lower-bound optimization above.

Ordinary greedy minimizes Uᵢ + αdᵢ, with Uᵢ = Nᵢ/μᵢ. Thus Uᵢ − minⱼ Uⱼ = αδᵢ(λ) would reproduce that optimized assignment. The smallest aggregate headcount compatible with these offsets is αΣᵢμᵢδᵢ(λ); Nᵢ includes any patient in service. A common congestion delay can sit above these offsets.

The theorem concerns critical-load weights. At subcritical arrival rates, using the newly optimized weights gives a static allocation benchmark, not a theorem about the exact stochastic mean wait or extra wait above shortest waiting. The static program allows loads equal to capacity; it does not ensure a stable stochastic system at its minimizing allocation. Nᵢ/μᵢ is a routing delay index rather than the exact conditional waiting time for lognormal service. Finite simulations may deviate from the benchmark.

Measurement and routing definitions
  • Queues. Nᵢ includes the patient in service. Qᵢ = max(Nᵢ − 1, 0) counts only waiting patients. Time averages are integrated exactly between events, not estimated from the plotted samples.
  • Warm-up. For horizon T and warm-up fraction w, time averages use [wT,T]. Waiting-time averages use all patients arriving in that window, including patients whose service begins after T. Arrivals stop at T; the queues are then drained without adding drain time to queue averages.
  • Service-time statistics. Each clinic's sample mean and sample SD use the complete service requirements of patients in the selected arrival cohort. Post-warm-up selection is by arrival time, not completion time. Sample moments are pooled across replications. The model mean is 1/μᵢ; the model SD is 1/μᵢ for exponential service and CV/μᵢ for lognormal service.
  • Routing. Fixed assignments are always honored. Flexible patients minimize Uᵢ = Nᵢ / μᵢ. Local patients minimize Uᵢ + α(dᵢ + δᵢ(λ)) under AC, with weights optimized at the entered arrival and service rates, Uᵢ + αdᵢ under ordinary greedy, (1 + 2κ)Uᵢ + αdᵢ under penalized greedy, and Uᵢ under shortest waiting. Every tie is broken uniformly. Shortest waiting uses the paper's observable delay index, rather than unobserved remaining workload.
  • Policy comparison. Compare all policies runs allocation-centered, ordinary greedy, shortest waiting, and penalized greedy at κ = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, and 8. Each row uses its own independent replication means for a Student-t confidence interval. Matching replication indices reuse common arrival, location, and clinic service streams across policies. The Detailed policy selector changes the clinic tables, travel breakdown, service statistics, queue path, and checks to that policy. Changing inputs retains the prior completed table and marks it as a previous comparison until rerun.
  • Demand and service. A Poisson arrival stream has rate λL + λC + λF. Each arrival is assigned a type independently in proportion to those rates. Local locations are uniform within the randomly chosen triangle; the density may jump across the diagonal. Service times have mean 1/μᵢ. For lognormal service, σ² = log(1 + CV²) and the log mean is log(1/μᵢ) − σ²/2.
  • Travel. Travel distance is the Euclidean distance from a local customer's sampled origin to the clinic selected by the policy; it is not multiplied by α or κ. The bottom and top averages classify local customers by origin, not destination. The all-customer mean divides the sum of local travel distances by all post-warm-up arrivals, assigning zero model travel to fixed and flexible customers, who have no sampled spatial origin. The all-local mean divides by the number of local arrivals only.
  • Travel optimization. The panel updates immediately from the current input rates, independently of the simulation. It minimizes E[destination distance | local] over spatial assignments and flexible flows satisfying λL qᵢ + λF θᵢ + fᵢ ≤ μᵢ and f₁ + f₂ = λC. For two clinics, the optimal local share q₁ is the nearest-clinic share clipped to the feasible interval; the corresponding distance-difference threshold is found from the continuous spatial distribution. The per-arrival objective multiplies the local mean by λL / (λL + λC + λF). Infeasible problems are shown as infeasible; no confidence interval is attached to a deterministic optimization value. The comparison with simulated travel is shown only when the simulation uses the same arrival rates, capacities, spatial demand, and fixed-destination probabilities.
  • Confidence intervals. With at least two valid independent replications, the reported interval is the mean of replication estimates ± t₀.₉₇₅,R−1 × sample standard deviation / √R. Total-queue intervals use each replication's total. No interval is estimated from dependent time samples. Undefined waiting, allocation or travel estimates from an empty cohort are omitted and their valid replication count is shown. A triangle with no local arrivals is undefined, rather than assigned distance zero. Intervals are not clipped at zero.
  • Checks. Full-run flow balance checks arrivals = departures by T + patients still in the system at T. A separate finite-window waiting-area check accounts for patients arriving before warm-up and waits extending past T. Travel checks reconcile origin counts and distance sums. These checks do not assume steady state.
All calculations run in this browser. No external libraries or network connection are required. Long or overloaded runs describe finite-horizon behavior; they need not represent steady state.
